Eating out without wrecking the plan

5 min read · Reviewed 14 May 2026 by the Diabest Friend nutrition team

A meal plan that only works in your own kitchen is not a plan that survives. Invitations, meetings, and lunches out will keep coming, what you can prepare is the set of small decisions you make on the spot.

At food stalls and restaurants

  • Ask for half the rice. Vendors hear it all the time, and it is the single change with the biggest effect.
  • Choose grilled, boiled, or steamed over deep-fried and heavy coconut-milk dishes.
  • Order vegetables as a separate dish (stir-fried greens, a clear soup, fresh salad) and eat them first.
  • Water or unsweetened tea. One glass of sweet iced tea can hold 20–30 grams of sugar, half a day's budget.
  • Chilli sauce is fine; crackers, sparingly. Prawn crackers feel light but their carbohydrate and oil are not.

Meal order costs nothing, and it works.

Vegetables and protein first, carbohydrate last. Without changing a single item on the plate, the post-meal rise tends to be lower.

At weddings and family gatherings

A long table with ten options is a test of volume, not of willpower. Three steps usually cover it:

  1. Do not arrive starving. Have a protein snack before you leave (a boiled egg, a handful of nuts) so your first choice is not the sweetest thing on the table.
  2. One trip only. Take everything you want to try on a single plate, in small portions. The second and third trips are the hard ones.
  3. Pick one sweet thing, not all of them. If you want the iced fruit dessert, skip the cake. Enjoying one thing deliberately lasts longer than banning everything.

At the office and the coffee shop

Sweet drinks are the biggest hidden sugar source of a working day. One palm-sugar latte can carry 25–40 grams of sugar without ever feeling like food. Order it unsweetened or half-sweet (nearly every shop obliges) and save the sweet version for occasions.

With meeting lunch boxes, eat the protein and vegetables and leave some of the rice. If there is fruit, it beats the cake at the back of the room.

Afterwards

A 10–15 minute walk after eating helps your muscles take up glucose. And if one meal did go too far, do not answer it by skipping the next; irregular eating makes blood sugar harder to predict. Just return to your usual schedule.

Medical note

If you use insulin or medication that can cause hypoglycaemia, never skip a meal without your doctor's guidance. Read the full disclaimer.
